What is Biophilic Design?

The science of connecting people with nature in built environments

The Biophilia Hypothesis

Coined by biologist E.O. Wilson, the "biophilia hypothesis" suggests that humans have an innate biological connection to nature developed over millions of years of evolution. When we're disconnected from natural elements in modern built environments, we experience increased stress, reduced focus, and diminished wellbeing.

"Biophilic design translates this innate human-nature connection into tangible architectural and interior design strategies that restore the health-giving benefits of nature exposure in our workplaces, homes, and public spaces."

Modern biophilic design goes far beyond aesthetics. It's rooted in environmental psychology, neuroscience, and evolutionary biology. Research from Harvard, Oxford, and leading design institutions demonstrates that nature exposure—whether through living plants, natural light, water features, or organic materials—triggers measurable physiological and psychological responses including:

  • Reduced cortisol levels (stress hormone) by up to 37%
  • Increased cognitive performance and attention restoration
  • Enhanced creativity and problem-solving abilities
  • Improved mood and emotional regulation
  • Faster recovery from mental fatigue

14 Patterns of Biophilic Design

Framework developed by Terrapin Bright Green

Visual Connection with Nature

Direct views to living plants, water, landscapes, or natural processes.

  • Indoor plants and green walls
  • Window views to gardens
  • Aquariums and water features

Non-Visual Connection with Nature

Auditory, tactile, olfactory, or gustatory stimuli.

  • Natural soundscapes (water, wind)
  • Natural textures (wood, stone)
  • Natural scents (flowers, herbs)

Dynamic & Diffuse Light

Changing light conditions mimicking natural daylight.

  • Natural daylight optimization
  • Varied lighting intensities
  • Shadow and light interplay

Presence of Water

Visual or auditory experience of water features.

  • Indoor fountains
  • Water walls and aquariums
  • Reflective pools

Biomorphic Forms & Patterns

Symbolic references to natural shapes, patterns, and textures.

  • Organic curved forms
  • Fractal patterns
  • Nature-inspired artwork

Material Connection with Nature

Use of natural materials and textures.

  • Wood, stone, bamboo
  • Natural fabrics and fibers
  • Earth-toned color palettes

Our Biophilic Design Process

Comprehensive consultation from assessment to implementation

Space Assessment & Analysis

We conduct thorough evaluation of your existing space including natural light availability, air circulation, acoustic challenges, employee work patterns, and spatial constraints. This includes understanding your organizational culture, wellness goals, and budget parameters.

Biophilic Strategy Development

Based on assessment findings, we develop a customized biophilic design strategy prioritizing interventions with highest impact. This includes selecting which of the 14 biophilic patterns to implement and creating phased implementation plans if needed.

Design Documentation & Visualization

Our in-house architects create detailed 3D renderings, technical drawings, and material specifications. You'll see exactly how natural elements will integrate with your existing architecture before any work begins.

Implementation & Installation

Our experienced installation teams execute the design with minimal disruption to your operations. We handle everything from structural modifications to plant installation, ensuring white-glove service throughout.

Post-Occupancy Evaluation

After implementation, we conduct follow-up assessments measuring employee satisfaction, space utilization, and biophilic element performance. Adjustments are made based on real-world feedback.

Ongoing Maintenance & Optimization

For natural elements, we offer comprehensive AMC services ensuring your biophilic installations remain vibrant and healthy. We monitor performance and suggest enhancements over time.
